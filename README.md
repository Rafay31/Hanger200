# Hanger200
<a name="top"></a>
A DIY hangboard cut on a CNC Router (inspired by the beastmaker fingerboards)

<img width="975" height="650" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/eee0a1c1-57fd-4fee-8cd5-5822dd65b097" />

This idea began when I realised I wanted a fingerboard to train for bouldering but commercial ones are a bit expensive. Considering that it was a really easy project to do, especially with already having the skillset and the tools to do so, I set out to make my own.

## The Process:

### Researching Edge Sizes:

I started by finding the edge sizes used in common fingerboards like the beastmaker 1000 and 2000.

<img width="1548" height="898" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/f4ce8da7-d69a-41ef-9f66-e3ada19a4f2b" />
Image Credits: https://www.atamanroman.dev/beastmaker-1000-2000-holds-and-edge-sizes/

<mark> Note that the edge sizes in my board are different to the ones shown in the above diagram <mark>

### Mockup in CAD:
I found a pre-existing model online ([Link](https://3dwarehouse.sketchup.com/model/uf5862615-5254-4e51-a0e1-8715ed2d2a9b/Beastmaker-2000-hangboard?hl=en)) and used it to judge roughly the dimensions of the holds as I did not have access to a physical board.

<mark> __Note:__ The router and milling bits I had access to could not have machined the board as a whole so I had to machine two halves seperately then glue and dowel them back together <mark>

I then proceeded to model [my own board](./Hanger200.STEP) in CAD and cut a foam test piece to check the toolpath

<img width="975" height="549" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/9c68e62b-b976-439d-888d-00c60be86eb7" />

### Cutting it out:

__Note:__ Most of this was cut using a 6mm 2F Carbide Ballnose bit. I would recommend using at least an 8mm if you want to speed things up from the several days it took to cut.

After confirming the toolpath was efficient and worked the top half was then cut out of beechwood.

<img width="4000" height="2250" alt="20260617_182503" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/db6f6eff-9277-4425-949e-4c518e3339fa" />

Then followed by the bottom half

<img width="4000" height="2250" alt="20260618_180442" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/c0159e6d-3324-45f8-91aa-0de73cc4f77e" />

After checking the alignment, both halves were glued together and 2 metal pins were hammered through to provide extra strength

<img width="4000" height="2250" alt="20260719_234624" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/95274e90-c8ab-458d-bb61-7be781a144a9" />

Leading to the final product (<a class="top-link" href="#top">Back to top</a> )

<mark> __Note:__ I would highly recommend a few coats of wood sealant (and stain if you want) if you live in a humid climate to avoid any moisture ingress to the wood which may cause strength degeneration and/or mold. <mark>
